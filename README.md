# Beyond the Horizon — Voyages of the Age of Exploration

An educational sailing game for the classroom. Students start on a map of the world **as Europeans pictured it around 1490**: Western Europe is fairly accurate, Africa and Asia are distorted, there's an imaginary southern continent and mythical islands, and the Americas are missing entirely. They choose one of four explorers and captain a caravel through **uncharted waters** to recreate that explorer's voyage.

| Explorer | Voyage | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Bartolomeu Dias** | 1487–1488 | Find the southern tip of Africa |
| **Christopher Columbus** | 1492–1493 | Reach "the Indies" by sailing west |
| **Vasco da Gama** | 1497–1498 | Sail around Africa to India |
| **Ferdinand Magellan** (and Elcano) | 1519–1522 | Sail west around the world to the Spice Islands |

Each completed voyage **redraws the world map** with what that explorer discovered. For example, Dias erases the imaginary southern land, Columbus adds the Caribbean, da Gama corrects India, and Magellan adds South America and the Pacific.

## How to play

- **Steer by the compass.** Arrow keys or WASD steer North, East, South and West. Press two at once for the in-between directions: ↓+← sails **southwest** and ↑+→ sails **northeast**. You can also tap a point on the compass rose, or click and hold on the sea.
- **Use the wind.** Sailing with the wind is fast and sailing straight into it barely moves you. The game uses the real wind belts (trade winds, westerlies, doldrums, monsoon), so historical routes like Columbus's trade-wind crossing and da Gama's *volta do mar* really are the fast way.
- **Keep your crew alive.** Food, water, health, morale and hull all run down at sea. Sail through **schools of fish** for food and under **rain squalls** for water. Reaching ports cures **scurvy**. In storms, press **Space** to **reef your sails**.
- **Explore.** The sea is covered in fog until you sail into it. Floating bottles hold **Codex scrolls** (key terms). Glowing wind streaks give a speed boost.
- **Learn at each landmark.** Each stop opens a journal page with an illustration, the history of that moment, key-term cards and a question from the pilot. Some stops are *Captain's Decisions* that show what really happened.
- **Pilot's Challenges** ask which compass direction leads to the next destination.
- **Report to the Crown.** After the voyage, the student reports to the monarch who paid for it: King John II (Dias), Ferdinand & Isabella (Columbus), King Manuel I (da Gama) or King Charles I (Elcano reports for Magellan). The monarch asks six recall questions, four about the explorer and two key terms from that voyage, then gives a verdict and a reward.

Other keys: **C** opens the chart, **P** or **Esc** pauses, **M** mutes, and **+**/**−** zoom.

## What students learn

- **Geography:** continents, oceans, seas, capes, straits, the Equator and the Tropics, latitude and longitude, plus the real coastlines of the routes.
- **Key terms:** 76 cards in the Navigator's Codex, sorted into Ships, Navigation, Winds, Places, People and Big Ideas. Examples include caravel, astrolabe, dead reckoning, trade winds, volta do mar, padrão, scurvy, Treaty of Tordesillas, circumnavigation and the Columbian Exchange.
- **People and outcomes:** each explorer's sponsor, ships, accomplishments and discoveries, and what happened next. This includes the Indigenous peoples they met (the Taíno, the Khoikhoi, Lapu-Lapu) and the consequences of the voyages.

## Class Race: Race to the Spice Islands

A whole-class game for the projector, opened with the **⚔ Class Race** button on the world map. The class splits into two crowns: **Portugal** sails east around Africa and **Spain** sails west across the Atlantic and Pacific, both racing to the Spice Islands, as they really did after the Treaty of Tordesillas.

- Each round shows one question to both teams. Questions come from the game's content: geography and compass directions, key terms, and explorers and events.
- Teams answer on mini whiteboards or through a captain. The teacher marks each team **Correct** or **Wrong**, then presses **Set Sail**.
- Right answers bring fair winds, and three in a row brings a trade-wind surge. Wrong answers still move the ship, just slowly, so no team falls hopelessly behind.
- Ships pass real milestones (Cape of Good Hope, Malindi, Strait of Magellan, the Pacific crossing and others). Each shows a short history card with a small boost or setback.
- Settings: team names, race length (10, 15 or 20 questions), question types, and an optional 30-second timer.
- Teacher keys: `1`/`2` Portugal right/wrong, `9`/`0` Spain right/wrong, `R` reveal the answer, `Enter` sail or next question.
- No accounts, internet service or student data are needed; everything runs on the one computer connected to the projector.

## Rewards that keep students playing

- **Stars (up to 3 per voyage):** one for finishing, one for answering 70% of the pilot's and the Crown's questions correctly, and one for finding 70% of the Codex scrolls.
- **Ducats:** spend them at the Royal Shipyard on better sails, hull, hold, navigation kit and crew. Upgrades carry over to every voyage.
- **Ranks:** Ship's Boy → Sailor → Pilot → Captain → Captain-Major → *Admiral of the Ocean Sea*.
- **The grand finale:** finishing all four voyages plays a short celebration:
  - the 1490 map redraws itself while the four routes are traced in order
  - the routes glow on a spinning globe
  - fireworks over the harbor
  - the student is named **Admiral of the Ocean Sea**, earns 500 bonus ducats and a golden caravel for every future voyage, and receives a royal decree certificate with their name to download or print

  The finale can be watched again from the **🏆 Honors** button.
- **Secret bonus expedition:** after the finale, a golden sun glyph appears in the New World on the map. Clicking it opens *The Other Shore: Tenochtitlan, 1519–1521*.
  - The student plays a young soldier on Cortés's expedition who keeps a chronicle. **There is no combat and nothing rewards conquest.**
  - Chapters: building the chain of interpreters (Malintzin and Aguilar); the march inland past Cempoala, Tlaxcala, Cholula and the volcanoes; exploring Tenochtitlan's temples, market, chinampas, causeways and aqueduct; and the fall of the city, told through Spanish and Nahua voices.
  - It ends with a "Why did Tenochtitlan fall?" reflection and an epilogue on the Nahua people and language today.
  - The hard history (the Cholula massacre, smallpox, the siege) is told honestly and without graphic detail.
  - It adds nine Codex terms and 200 ducats.
- **Saving:** progress is saved in the browser automatically. Voyages save at every landmark, so a student can stop at the end of class and continue next time.

## Practice deck

`practice/Beyond-the-Horizon-Practice.pptx` is a 40-slide PowerPoint that holds all of the practice for the unit, illustrated with art from the game. Students type their answers into the dashed boxes.

Activities climb in difficulty:
1. **Recall:** word banks, matching, Who Am I?, an explorer fact table and fill-in-the-blank.
2. **Sort:** God, Gold or Glory; Portugal or Spain; navigation tools; cause or effect.
3. **Geography lab (10 slides):** compass directions, continents and oceans, key places, latitude and longitude, wind belts, tracing routes, the 1490 vs. 1522 maps, and reading the in-game instruments.
4. **Events:** a timeline, cause-and-effect chains, and matching game scenes to events.
5. **Primary sources:** seven excerpts from published translations (Zurara, Columbus's journal and letter, da Gama's *Roteiro*, the Treaty of Tordesillas, Pigafetta, Las Casas), each with sourcing and close-reading questions, plus a corroboration task.
6. **Claims:** a claim–evidence–reasoning model and two writing tasks, including a counterclaim.

Answers are in each slide's speaker notes and on two answer-key slides.

## Lesson presentation

`practice/Beyond-the-Horizon-Lessons.pptx` is a 37-slide teaching deck that comes before the game and the practice deck. It is split into five segments: Why Sail?; Ships, Stars and Winds; Around Africa; Across the Atlantic; Around the World. Each segment ends with a game voyage and the matching practice slides.

- **Built on Mayer's multimedia principles:** maps and pictures carry the content, labels sit next to what they name, each slide makes one point, key terms come first, and the explanation is spoken from the notes instead of printed on the slide.
- **Built on Bjork's desirable difficulties:** students predict before each reveal (the map pretest, the Columbus route), recall with notes closed at spaced points, and answer mixed questions about several explorers.
- **Student participation:** badges mark each Turn & Talk, Check (hinge question), Predict, Recall and Write activity.
- **Primary sources:** da Gama's *Roteiro*, Columbus's journal, the Treaty of Tordesillas and Pigafetta, each with sourcing questions.
- **Teacher notes on every slide:** a script, timing, answers, common misconceptions and the reason for each design choice.

## Running it

This is a plain static website with no build step and no server code. All graphics, music and sound effects are generated in the browser.

- **Locally:** open `index.html` in a modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari). The period fonts load from Google Fonts when online and fall back to serif fonts offline.
- **GitHub Pages:** in the repository go to *Settings → Pages* and deploy from the branch. Students then open `https://<user>.github.io/<repo>/`.
- Works with keyboard, mouse or touch, on Chromebooks, laptops and tablets.
